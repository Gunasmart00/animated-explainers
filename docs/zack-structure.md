# What we copy from Zack D. Films (studied 2026-10-24)

Sources: https://zackdfilms.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Shorts (524 shorts) and Category:Characters (91 characters).
We copy the **format and look**, never his characters, titles or scripts. His story ideas can inspire us now and then.

## 1. The look (replaces our old cartoon style; owner 2026-10-24)
Watched the owner's 7 sample shorts (`Downloads\zack samples`, 2026-10-24): his videos look like a **real-time video-game
engine render** (an Unreal Engine cutscene with Character-Creator-style people): realistic proportions, smooth slightly
plastic game-character skin, simple hair, a little stiff and uncanny; plain game-level rooms (sky-blue painted walls,
wood floors, basic props); bright flat lighting; wide-angle camera; big emotional faces (tears) at story peaks; floating
red numbers/timers and lime-free captions. Not cinematic, not Pixar, not a photo. (Owner rejected both our old cartoon
look and photo-real Canva dogs on 2026-10-24.)

Our style block (use in every Flow prompt; rewritten 2026-10-24 after studying 35 frames from the owner's 7 samples
next to our Bobbie frames, which came out too cinematic: golden hour, night, lanterns, rich wood, soft focus, sentimental faces):
> IMPORTANT: realistic 3D video-game graphics, NOT a cartoon and NOT an animated movie: adults have realistic adult anatomy, realistic head shapes and realistic small eyes (no big cartoon eyes, no rounded cartoon faces, no exaggerated expressions beyond what a real person does). 3D animation that looks like a real-time video-game engine render (an Unreal Engine cutscene), the look of popular 3D explainer shorts. People: realistic proportions with video-game character faces (smooth, slightly plastic skin, simple realistic hair, natural-sized eyes, a little stiff, big clear expressions), plain solid-colour clothes (T-shirts in white, orange, yellow, red). Animals: realistic game-asset models with simplified fur. Sets: simple game-level spaces built from plain, solid-coloured surfaces (blue-grey, sky-blue or teal painted walls, white ceilings, light wood or grey floors), low-detail textures, only a few props, often a single ceiling spotlight. Light: clean, neutral-to-cool and even, like a bright game level, soft short shadows; no golden hour, no warm candle or lantern glow, no lens flares, no light rays, no fog or haze. Everything in sharp focus (no depth of field, no film grain). Colours: a cool blue-dominant palette (blue, teal, navy, white) with one warm accent colour (orange, red or yellow) on the main character or object. Camera: slightly wide-angle; high three-quarter views, close-ups on hands and objects, top-down shots. Not a photograph, not cinematic film lighting, not Pixar or cartoon style, not anime, no text anywhere.

What his frames look like (35 sample frames, 2026-10-24):
- **Palette:** blue everywhere: blue-grey hotel walls, navy studios, teal hallways, deep-blue ocean. Warm colour only on one thing (a red bellhop vest, an orange T-shirt, a yellow shirt).
- **Sets:** empty game levels: a hotel room with one spotlight, a hallway with teal walls and a wood floor, a lab with a green table. Few props, flat textures.
- **People:** Character-Creator faces, slightly stiff, plain T-shirts and jeans; big readable expressions (shock, worry).
- **Explaining shots:** a single object floating on a plain light-blue gradient background (two dollar bills; the Earth on black). Use these for numbers and facts.
- **Camera:** high three-quarter angles, close on hands (passing money, a wallet), top-down, the occasional low angle.
- **Graphics (added in editing, not in Flow):** flip counters ("1807 HOURS"), a red digital timer, a red question mark, a thought bubble with emoji icons, a glowing red outline of a person. These are our pop-ups.
- **Night** only when the story needs it, as a bright blue game-style night with clearly lit faces.

Do not use his frames as Flow image inputs (they are his work, and Veo would copy his characters and sets). Use our own approved frames as references instead.

## 2. The writing formula (from his story-telling shorts)
Examples: "He Hid In The Jungle For 30 Years 😱" (0:38, 59M views), "Bullied Boy's Shirt Became Official College
Gear 🥲" (7.5M views).
- **80–110 words, 35–45 s.** One narrator, third person ("he", "she"); names are optional and often left out.
- **Open in the middle of the most visual moment**, no question, no "meet X":
  "Thousands of leaflets dropped from the sky…" / "He drew a logo, then proudly clipped it onto his orange shirt…"
- **Pivot words** carry the story: "But…", "You see,…", "So…", "Well,…", "Finally,…".
- **The ending calls back the opening object**, flipped: "…the same shirt he was mocked for was now worn across the
  country." / "…finally left the war that had ended 30 years prior."
- **Title = short sentence + one emoji** that sets the feeling: 😱 😨 🤔 😮 🥲 😏.
- Touching stories are allowed when the **ending is uplifting** (our "never sad" rule = the ending, not every beat).
- Our extra rules still apply on top: hook in 1 s, emotion by 3 s, Easter egg, twist in the last second, loop.

## 3. The page structure (one page per short)
Every short on the wiki has the same page; ours lives in `episodes/<name>/README.md` (template:
`templates/short-page.md`):
1. **Infobox:** link, release date, duration, type, views, likes, comments.
2. **Shorts guide:** previous short / next short.
3. **One-line summary.**
4. **Characters, objects, and locations** (three lists).
5. **Transcript** (the narration).
6. **Trivia** ("based on a true story", sources).
7. **Gallery** (cover / key frames).
8. **References.**

Types (categories): **Story-telling** (true stories), **Fictional story-telling**, **Fact-telling**, **How it works**.
Index of all our shorts: `docs/shorts-index.md`. Recurring cast with fixed looks: `docs/characters.md`.
