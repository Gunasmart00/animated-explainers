**Summary:** In August 2014 a woman at a Florida coffee drive-through paid for the car behind her, and 378 strangers kept
the chain going for 11 hours, until one woman simply paid for her own coffee and drove off.

## Characters, objects, and locations
- **Location:** a small teal drive-through coffee kiosk (no signs or logos), palm trees, blue sky.
- **Characters:** the barista (mid-20s, black curly hair, short beard, teal cap, white T-shirt, navy apron); the first
  driver (orange T-shirt, red hatchback); the second driver (blonde bob, yellow T-shirt, light-blue sedan); the last
  driver (grey bun, big sunglasses, mint-green car); a second barista (black ponytail).
- **Objects:** plain white cups, a card, coins, white marker tally marks on the window.

## Transcript (v2, after detailed research)
A woman at a coffee drive-through paid for her iced coffee... and then for the stranger's caramel macchiato in the car behind her.
So that driver paid for the next car. And that driver paid for the one after that.
The baristas started counting, and told every driver how long the chain was.
You see, now nobody wanted to be the one who broke it.
For eleven hours, car after car kept paying. 378 people in a row.
Finally, at six in the evening, a woman pulled up, heard her coffee was already paid for...
and just paid for her own, and drove off.

## Trivia
- Based on a true story: Starbucks, St. Petersburg, Florida, 20 Aug 2014, 7 AM to 6 PM. (The brand is named only in

## Research notes (2026-10-24)
- Store: the Starbucks on Tyrone Boulevard, St. Petersburg, Florida; Wednesday 20 Aug 2014, from about 7 AM (Patch, Tampa Bay Times).
- The first woman paid for her iced coffee and the stranger's caramel macchiato behind her (Tampa Bay Times summary).
- Staff counted and told each customer how many had paid it forward before them (shift supervisor, on NPR).
- A man said he joined because he "didn't want to be the jerk to end the line" (NPR).
- 378 people; the store's old record was 141 (Patch). Customer 379 declined at 6 PM and paid only for her own $2.25 drink (NBC).
- NPR mentions "more than 450" including later; we use 378 (NBC, Fox, Patch agree).
- Flow lessons: C1 v1 car drove nose-first at the window; C1 v2 the red car rolled backwards and faces were cartoon. Fix: lock the look and geometry in a free start image first, keep cars still, one simple camera move.
