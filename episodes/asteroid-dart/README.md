**Summary:** On 26 Sep 2022, NASA's DART spacecraft (vending-machine size, about 1,260 lb) flew about 7 million miles
and hit the harmless asteroid moonlet Dimorphos (about 525 ft across) at about 14,000 mph. The goal was to nudge it,
not blow it up. Success meant shortening its 11 h 55 min orbit around Didymos by at least 73 seconds; it shortened by
32 minutes (to 11 h 23 min), partly because the blasted-off debris acted like a rocket. ESA's Hera spacecraft arrives
at the pair in November 2026 to inspect the result.

## Core-object geometry (research)
- **DART:** a box about 1.2 × 1.3 × 1.3 m (vending-machine size); two long, thin rectangular roll-out solar panels
  (about 8.5 m each) sticking out on opposite sides; a round dish antenna; one camera on the front face.
- **Didymos:** about 780 m wide, shaped like a spinning top (a ridge around its middle), grey rubble.
- **Dimorphos:** about 160 m (525 ft), egg-shaped pile of loose grey boulders, circles Didymos like a small moon.
- **LICIACube:** a shoebox-sized cubesat with two small solar wings, following a few minutes behind.
- After impact: a huge fan-shaped plume of dust and boulders, later a long comet-like tail.
