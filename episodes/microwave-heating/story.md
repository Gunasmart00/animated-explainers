# How does a microwave heat food?

From the idea tracker (youtube_clipping/templates/trackers/explainer-ideas.csv).

- The magnetron produces microwaves (about 2.45 GHz in home ovens).
- Metal walls reflect them; the door mesh holes are smaller than the wavelength, so they stay in.
- Water molecules are polar. The field flips billions of times a second; molecules rotate to follow it,
  and that motion spreads to neighbours as heat.
- Wet food heats fast; dry ceramic barely heats.
- Standing waves make hot and cold spots; the turntable moves food through them.
