**Summary:** In 2017, whale biologist Nan Hauser (63, 28 years studying whales) was filming humpbacks off Muri Beach,
Rarotonga, Cook Islands. A humpback of about 23 tonnes kept pushing her with its head and mouth for over ten minutes,
tucked her under its pectoral fin and lifted her out of the water once. She was scared and bruised. Back at the boat she
saw a large tiger shark (about 4.5 m) nearby, while a second whale slapped its tail. She believes the whale was
protecting her. One year and 15 days later, a whale swam up to her boat, ignored everyone else and nudged her: two
notches in its tail fluke and a scar on its head, like the whale that saved her.

**Honesty note:** some scientists doubt it was protection (National Geographic). The narration says "she thought" and
"you see, humpbacks have been seen protecting...", never "scientists proved".

## Core-object geometry (research, 2026-10-25)
- **Humpback:** about 14 m long (≈ 8.5 × a 1.65 m woman). Pectoral fins up to a third of body length (about 4.5 m,
  nearly 3 × her height), white on South Pacific humpbacks, with a bumpy front edge. Knobbly bumps (tubercles) on the
  head and jaw; long grooves on the white throat and belly; small dorsal fin; tail fluke about 4.5 m wide, each one
  with a unique trailing-edge pattern.
- **Tiger shark:** about 4.5 m; blunt, wide, square snout; faint dark vertical stripes on a grey-brown back; white belly.
- **The scientist (invented look):** a woman about 63, short silver-grey hair, black wetsuit, clear mask, bright orange
  snorkel and fins (the warm accent in a blue world).
- **Seals and killer whales (cutaway):** in Antarctica in 2009 a humpback was photographed with a seal on its chest,
  out of reach of killer whales.
