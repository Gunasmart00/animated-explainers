## 2. Narration (for review, ~114 words, ~47 s at Mark's pace)
1. Why is McDonald's ice cream machine always broken?
2. At any moment, more than one in ten in the US can't serve ice cream, and a website tracks it live.
3. Here's the secret: milk is a feast for bacteria.
4. So every night, the machine cooks itself clean: it heats the mix to kill the germs, then cools it back down.
5. That can take up to four hours.
6. And if one step fails, it locks itself, and only a technician with a secret code can unlock it.
7. So "broken" usually means "cleaning"... or "waiting for that one guy."
8. It got so bad, the US government stepped in.
9. In 2024, it finally became legal for restaurants to fix their own machines.

Loop: "...fix their own machines." runs into "Why is McDonald's ice cream machine always broken?"

## 3. Visual plan (one continuous take; prompts after the voice)
- **No brand marks in Flow:** a generic, bright fast-food restaurant (red and yellow, no logos, no golden arches, no
  text). The brand is only in the narration.
- **Hook (0–3 s):** wide shot of the busy restaurant zooming to the counter: a kid's excited face drops as the worker
  shrugs at the soft-serve machine (comic disappointment, not sad).
- **Easter egg:** a small sand hourglass sits on top of the machine from the first frame; it's the cleaning timer,
  paid off when the cycle is explained and when the "secret code" panel opens.
- **Progression:** a dotted tracker map lighting up broken machines; x-ray cutaway of the machine: milk mix glowing
  hot (germs fleeing like cartoon microbes), then frosty cool; a 4-hour sweep around the hourglass; a padlock
  snaps shut on the panel and a technician silhouette with a glowing keypad.
- **Climax:** the "BROKEN" sign flips to show the machine happily cleaning itself (no text: a sleeping-machine icon).
- **Twist (last second):** a giant official gavel/stamp slams down and the padlock pops open; the worker grins and
  grabs a wrench; pull back out to the wide restaurant (= first frame) for the loop.
