# What Happens When You Stop Drinking Water

Owner's brief (2026-10-18): show what happens inside the body as dehydration gets worse, from the first thirst to
dangerous organ stress. Hook: "The moment you stop drinking water, your body starts fighting to survive." Make it
realistic (accurate) and easy to understand visually.

Checked but NOT used:
- "Dehydration shrinks your brain": a 2.2% body-mass loss expanded the brain's fluid spaces (ventricles) on MRI, but
  total brain volume didn't measurably change ([Kempton et al. 2009](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/2566)). So the video only
  says water is pulled out of cells, including in the brain, in severe dehydration.
- Exact heart-rate or temperature rise per 1% of water lost: the figures vary between studies, so the video doesn't
  give a number.

## Visual facts for the animation (real-world sizes, positions)
- **The girl:** adult woman, 1.60 m tall. **The boyfriend:** 1.75 m tall; she comes up to about his chin.
- **Water bottle:** 750 ml, about 24 cm tall.
- **Brain:** inside the skull. The **hypothalamus** is about the size of an almond, at the base of the brain above the
  brainstem. The **pituitary** is pea-sized, hanging directly below it on a short stalk
  ([Cleveland Clinic: Hypothalamus](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22566-hypothalamus),
  [Posterior pituitary](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/23150-posterior-pituitary)).
- **Kidneys:** two bean-shaped organs about the size of a fist, against the back wall of the abdomen on either side of
  the spine, just below the ribs ([NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidneys-how-they-work)).
  Each has about a million filters (nephrons). Together they filter about 150 quarts (about 140 L) of blood a day and make
  only 1–2 quarts of urine.
- **Heart:** about the size of a fist, in the middle of the chest, tilted slightly left, behind the breastbone.
- **Red blood cells:** microscopic (about 7–8 µm), so they are shown only in a macro view inside a blood vessel, never
  next to whole organs.
- **Urine colour:** pale straw is healthy; dark yellow or amber means dehydrated
  ([Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/9013-dehydration)).
- **Severe stage:** shown with care. No death on screen; the "3 days" beat uses a time-lapse of the sun.
