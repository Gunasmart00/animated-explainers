# D.B. Cooper: the man who vanished mid-flight

Researched 2026-10-18. Sources at the bottom; every fact below is in at least two of them unless marked.

## The story
- **Wednesday 24 November 1971, the day before Thanksgiving.** A quiet man in his mid-forties, wearing a dark
  business suit, white shirt and black tie, buys a one-way ticket in cash from Portland, Oregon, to Seattle
  under the name **"Dan Cooper"**. Northwest Orient Airlines **Flight 305**, a Boeing 727. He sits in the last row
  (seat 18-E). [FBI, Wikipedia, HistoryLink]
- He orders a **bourbon and soda**. Shortly after takeoff he hands flight attendant Florence Schaffner a **note**
  saying he has a **bomb** in his briefcase. He opens it: red cylinders wired to a battery. [FBI, Wikipedia, HistoryLink]
- His demand: **$200,000 in $20 bills and four parachutes**, delivered in Seattle. [FBI, Wikipedia, HistoryLink]
- The plane lands in Seattle at 5:46 pm. He swaps the **36 passengers** for the money and parachutes, keeps the
  crew, and orders the plane to fly toward Mexico City via Reno, low and slow, with the rear stairs down. [FBI, Wikipedia]
- **A little after 8 pm**, somewhere over the forests of southwest Washington, on a dark, rainy night, he jumps from
  the **rear airstair** with the money. The plane lands in Reno; he's gone. [FBI, Wikipedia, HistoryLink]
- The FBI searched for years. **Nothing** from the jump was found. [Wikipedia, HistoryLink]
- **10 February 1980:** 8-year-old **Brian Ingram**, smoothing sand for a campfire at Tena Bar on the Columbia River,
  finds rotting bundles of $20 bills: **$5,800**. The serial numbers match the ransom. The rest of the money, and
  Cooper, were never found. [FBI, HistoryLink, Wikipedia]
- The name **"D.B." was a press mistake**; he called himself Dan Cooper. [FBI, Wikipedia]
- In **2016** the FBI stopped actively investigating. It's the only unsolved hijacking in commercial aviation
  history. [FBI, Wikipedia, HistoryLink]
- **Payoff:** after more hijackers copied him in 1972, the FAA ordered Boeing 727s fitted with a small spring-loaded
  flap that stops the rear stairs being lowered in flight — the **Cooper vane**. [Wikipedia: Cooper vane, Traveller]

## Visual facts for the animation (researched 2026-10-18; prompts must follow these)
Sources: Wikipedia "D. B. Cooper"; FBI "D.B. Cooper Hijacking"; USPA Parachutist "The Secrets of D.B. Cooper, part one";
This Day in Aviation "24 November 1971"; Wikipedia "Cooper vane"; AeroCorner "D.B. Cooper, the 727 and the Cooper vane".
- **The plane:** Northwest Orient Boeing 727-51 (N467US). A 727 is a trijet: **no engines under the wings**; two engine
  pods on the sides of the rear fuselage and a third inside the base of the tall T-tail (S-shaped intake on top of the
  fuselage in front of the fin). The **ventral airstair** drops from the underside of the tail cone, beneath the tail
  engines. Show it white with a red tail; never show the airline name or logo.
- **Cooper's look:** mid-40s, dark business suit, white shirt, thin black clip-on tie with a gold/pearl tie clip, black
  raincoat, slip-on loafers, **dark sunglasses** worn in the airport and on the plane (USPA), black attaché case.
  Sat in the last row (18-E).
- **The note:** given shortly after takeoff to flight attendant Florence Schaffner, who sat in the jump seat behind
  him; she first put it in her purse unread. **Bomb:** a large cylindrical battery wired to two rows of four red
  cylinders in the attaché case.
- **Ransom:** 10,000 unmarked $20 bills (about 21 lb) delivered in a **cloth bank bag** (he had asked for a knapsack).
  **Parachutes:** four — two backpacks and two chest reserves (plain nylon military/sport rigs, olive/tan/white).
- **Seattle:** landed after dark (5:39–5:46 pm) on a remote, poorly lit part of the airfield; window shades closed;
  rain. A Northwest employee (Al Lee, in civilian clothes) brought the money and parachutes; flight attendant Tina
  Mucklow fetched them at the door (Wikipedia: front door; This Day in Aviation: aft stairs) and carried them to Cooper.
  **Passengers left by mobile boarding stairs at the front passenger door** (forward left side of the fuselage, not
  the nose). Three fuel trucks refuelled the plane.
- **Take-off (7:40 pm):** at night in rain; aft stairs **closed** for take-off (crew refused to take off with them
  down). Flown slowly (about 170 knots), under 10,000 ft, **landing gear down, flaps at 15°**, cabin unpressurised.
- **The jump (about 8:13 pm):** the aft-stair warning light came on about 8:00 pm. Dark night, heavy rain, about
  20°F, over dense forest of tall firs. He wore a backpack parachute and a chest reserve over his suit, tied the money
  bag around his waist with cord cut from a reserve parachute, walked down the rear stairs in loafers holding the
  handrails, and jumped. He left his black clip-on tie with the tie clip on seat 18-E.
- **1980 find:** 8-year-old Brian Ingram was raking the sandy riverbank at Tena Bar on the Columbia River to make a
  campfire and uncovered three rotting packets of $20 bills held by rubber bands (about $5,800).
- **Cooper vane:** a small spring-loaded metal paddle beside the aft airstair under the tail. On the ground the spring
  holds it out, square to the fuselage, and the stairs can open; in flight the airflow pushes it flat against the
  fuselage, which swings a plate under the stairs and locks them shut.

### Corrections from the owner (2026-10-18, with a reference photo of a parked 727)
- The jump stairway (ventral airstair) comes **out of the belly under the tail**, on the centre line, behind the wings and
  below the centre engine. Lowered, it slopes **down and backwards** from the belly to the ground (or into the air
  in flight). There is **no side door** involved. Cooper walked **down** it and jumped off its lower end.
- Our first clip 9 drew a side door near the tail with an ordinary boarding staircase, and our first clip 13 drew stairs
  that looked like they went up. Both are now ruled out in the scenes' `never:` lists, and clip 9 starts from clip 8's
  frame, which already shows the belly stairway correctly.
- The Reel must loop: clip 13 ends on clip 1's first frame (the paddle under the belly, sunny ramp).
